# Order-Parameter-First Fundamental Frame

- **Status:** canonical project interpretation
- **Adopted:** 2026-07-25
- **Historical execution snapshot:** `23480d16f86f4e5616fa77c5b7ff93b2d6a5469d`
- **Decision made after the subject outcome generated from that snapshot:**
  `true`
- **Prior program:** pair-first structural retrieval and loop/transport
  instrumentation
- **Applies to:** project ontology, claim labels, and new experiment design from
  the commit that first tracks this document
- **Does not alter:** any earlier protocol, freeze, receipt, audit, reservation
  marker, or observed outcome

This document changes the fundamental interpretation of SpiralLens. It is not
an extra null attached to the historical pair-first structural-retrieval
program or its intended loop, relative-holonomy, and sampled-winding
integration. SpiralLens now treats a model-derived order parameter, when one
is claimed, as an explicit scientific object that must be defined before a
core, phase, winding, or topological-defect interpretation can exist.

The change is intentionally prospective and historically explicit. It was
adopted after the subject outcome generated from the snapshot above; it is not
represented as having governed earlier frozen runs. Earlier artifacts retain
their original bytes and contracts. The
[Experiment Interpretation Ledger](EXPERIMENT_INTERPRETATION_LEDGER.md)
records both their original purpose and their position under this frame.

This is a post-outcome conceptual revision and a stricter interpretation, not
an outcome-driven rescue. It changes no observed threshold, does not authorize
a rerun, and cannot use an earlier Pythia outcome to select a future field,
graph, core, or charge definition. The earlier audit did not test this new
question and is neither supporting nor falsifying evidence for it.

## Authority and precedence

The project keeps execution, observation, interpretation, and planning
authority separate:

1. a frozen protocol and execution freeze control how one run may execute;
2. a persisted artifact or observation-only witness controls what was
   observed;
3. this Fundamental Frame controls current ontology and future admissible
   labels;
4. the Claim Taxonomy controls evidence levels;
5. the Interpretation Ledger records dated readings and amendments;
6. the Roadmap controls prospective implementation order.

A later item cannot mutate or promote an earlier protocol or observation. A
Roadmap step cannot authorize execution, and a ledger entry cannot supply
missing evidence.

## 1. Fundamental question

Before this frame, the declared question asked whether transformer
representations contained reproducible loop, relative-holonomy, or
sampled-winding candidates after the stated controls. The executable path was
still pair-first retrieval with candidate-to-loop integration intended later.
This frame preserves that work in the geometry branch and separately adds the
positive referent obligations needed for a defect interpretation:

> On a declared observation substrate, can SpiralLens define and replay either
> a geometric transport observable or a model-derived field with a singular
> set, and can the resulting claim survive the controls appropriate to its
> mathematical type?

The optical-vortex analogy is load-bearing only for the second branch, and
only after its substrate referents have been specified. A robust continuous
rotation remains a legitimate geometric result even when no order parameter,
core, or quantized charge exists.

## 2. Fundamental object stack

A claim-bearing run must keep the following objects distinct.

### 2.1 Observation substrate

The substrate \(X\) is the declared domain on which quantities are observed.
Its identity includes at least:

- model ID and immutable revision;
- ContextBank, role, and selected context;
- sweep and observation positions;
- layer or other evolution axis;
- canonical row universe;
- state and update tensors;
- metric, preprocessing, and any neighborhood construction used to estimate a
  local field.

A token ID, a decoded string, a projected plot, and an ANN index are not a
substrate definition by themselves.

### 2.2 State or response

At each admissible observation \(x\in X\), a protocol may define a state,
update, Jacobian response, local covariance, projector, or another typed
quantity. These are measurements from which a field might be constructed.
They are not automatically an order parameter.

### 2.3 Order parameter or section

An order parameter is a protocol-bound field or section
\(\psi\) with all of the following declared:

- its domain and codomain or target manifold;
- its construction and any fitted parameters;
- its fit scope and leakage boundary;
- its gauge or basis transformation law;
- its amplitude or identifiability quantity;
- the condition under which its normalized direction is defined;
- its interpolation on a declared discrete substrate;
- its content and provenance identity.

Amplitude and phase-like direction must arise from the same declared object.
Combining an effective-rank scalar from one construction with a Procrustes
angle from another does not create a complex order parameter.

Coordinates expressed in vertex-wise local frames are not globally comparable
by default. If \(U_x\) varies with \(x\), raw differences of
\(\arg(U_x^\top s_x)\) are gauge-dependent. A cross-vertex angular increment
must use the declared connection or a globally frozen frame, and the
transformation law of the combined increment must be tested.

The existing raw-array winding API is a mathematical primitive. Supplying it
with an array does not establish that the array is a model-derived order
parameter.

The provisional pointwise-referent contract now makes one prerequisite of this
frame executable. It declares that F0 support and the F1 projector are not
order parameters; F2 and F3 obtain amplitude and direction from the same
pointwise vector; and F4 obtains both from the same pointwise traceless
spin-two tensor under a distinct doubled-angle convention. The contract is
bound to the P0 registry by content digest and explicitly leaves substrate
field and interpolation binding false. A pointwise fiber formula is not an
order parameter under the definition above. The discrete domain,
interpolation, matched-cycle, model-side construct-validity, and promotion
evidence remain separate obligations.

### 2.4 Connection and transport

A connection describes how a vector, frame, or subspace is transported between
observations. Its closed-loop holonomy is generally continuous and may be
meaningful without an order parameter or defect.

Connection holonomy and order-parameter winding remain separate observables.
Neither is converted into the other by naming a rotation angle “phase.”

### 2.5 Singular or defect set

For a complex scalar field, a candidate singular set \(Z\) may be where
\(|\psi|\) reaches a protocol-defined zero or unresolved interval. For a
subspace or tensor field, the analogous event may be a loss of
identifiability, such as a frozen spectral-gap closure, rather than literal
zero norm.

A low anisotropy, effective rank, projected norm, density, or coherence value
is a field-unbound `SupportDiagnostic`. It becomes a `CoreScore` only when it
binds the exact `OrderParameterSpec` and field plus a frozen rule explaining
why that score makes the normalized order parameter zero or undefined. A
sparse or missing-data region is not a core.

Core localization must be independent of the charge evaluation. A core cannot
be defined after observing which center maximizes winding.

### 2.6 Loop or cycle class

A cycle is not identified only by its index in one graph's cycle basis. A
topological comparison must bind a matched class, support, or anchor rule that
can be followed across admissible graph and interpolation constructions.

On a generic neighbor graph there may be no canonical inside, outside, face
orientation, or localized point core. In that case SpiralLens may report a
sampled graph-cycle winding or cohomology index, but not a localized
topological charge around a core.

The provisional graph/domain foundation makes a narrower prerequisite
executable without weakening this rule. A graph-independent
`DiscreteDomainComplex` verifies an exact finite integer chain complex, and a
`BoundaryCycleClassSpec` may declare the oriented boundary induced by an exact
face support. A `CycleClassBinding` then proves only that a graph cycle refines
that same combinatorial boundary exactly once. It does not infer faces from a
neighbor graph, prove latent-manifold topology, claim a nontrivial homology
class, or identify the support boundary with a core. The records read no field
or loop observable, but they do not certify the caller's prior selection
history or outcome blindness. A later sealed protocol must supply that
provenance. Common-boundary availability across graph families is not yet
graph-family cycle invariance, and these records do not advance D4.

## 3. Two scientific branches

### 3.1 Geometry and transport branch

This branch contains:

- local frames or projectors;
- JVP, pullback, whitening, and Procrustes choices;
- continuous connection and relative holonomy;
- architecture-factor accounting;
- gauge-covariant or conjugacy-invariant readouts;
- reverse, radius, sampling, and matched geometric nulls.

It does not require a vanishing amplitude or integer charge. A successful
result is an invariant geometric or transport candidate, not a vortex.

### 3.2 Field and defect branch

This branch additionally requires:

- a substrate-bound order-parameter specification;
- a replayable field artifact;
- an independently localized singular or unresolved set;
- nonzero amplitude or adequate identifiability on every evaluated boundary;
- a declared target-space topology and corresponding charge group;
- branch and sampling resolution;
- matched cycle classes across an admissible construction family;
- deformation and graph-family invariance.

Only this branch can produce a topological-defect candidate. Even then,
“optical vortex,” “OAM,” and semantic interpretation remain outside the claim
unless separately established.

### 3.3 Joint interpretation

A geometric candidate and a defect candidate may later be joined only when
they bind the same substrate, field, core support, loop class, and protocol.
Agreement is evidence to evaluate; it is not guaranteed by construction.

No persisted type named `VortexCandidate` is introduced until the topological
branch has passed analytic qualification and an independently reviewed
promotion contract.

## 4. Candidate order-parameter families

The project does not select a Pythia order parameter in this document. It
defines the hypotheses that independent calibration may compare.

### 4.1 Oriented two-channel section

Given a frozen, independently fitted oriented rank-two frame \(U_x\) and a
declared response \(s_x\), define

\[
z_x = U_x^\top s_x \in \mathbb{R}^2 \simeq \mathbb{C}.
\]

Its amplitude and angular direction come from the same section. Its risks are
projection leakage, plane instability, reflection ambiguity, and artificial
zeros. It remains exploratory unless the frame is fit without subject
loop/winding/charge outcomes and survives the declared basis, random-plane,
and held-out controls.

### 4.2 Two-dimensional tensor or director field

For a declared symmetric two-dimensional response tensor \(T_x\), an
illustrative nematic order parameter is

\[
\psi_T(x)=(T_{11}-T_{22})+2iT_{12}.
\]

Here \(|\psi_T|\) is the eigenvalue gap and the phase is twice the principal
axis angle. At isotropy the direction becomes undefined. This construction is
mathematically coherent, but it carries a director rather than ordinary vector
convention and may admit half-charge interpretations before the doubled-angle
encoding. The tensor, probe plane, estimator, and convention must all be
frozen.

### 4.3 Rank-two projector bundle

A local rank-two projector \(P_x=U_xU_x^\top\), principal-angle coherence, and
Procrustes connection provide a gauge-accounted geometric field. Effective
rank, concentration, and spectral gaps can gate whether the plane is
identifiable.

The projector alone has no complex phase and no integer winding. A sampled
integer requires an additional nonzero, orientable section. If the bundle is
non-orientable, a forced proper rotation is forbidden; the U(1) charge is
`insufficient` or undefined rather than manufactured.

Orientability and nonzero amplitude are necessary but not sufficient for a
local-frame integer. The protocol must additionally freeze a global
trivialization/reference or a gauge-invariant connection-corrected lift and
show how continuous connection holonomy is kept separate. Without that
construction, the eligible output remains continuous geometry.

These families are competing mechanisms. Agreement cannot be assumed, and
their outputs must not be pooled into one positive label.

## 5. Quantization boundary

Quantization follows from the target and its admissible non-singular space; it
is not produced by rounding a continuous rotation.

- A single-valued nonzero complex scalar on a loop defines a map to \(S^1\)
  and supports an integer winding of the declared interpolation.
- A director or projective field has a different orientation convention and
  potentially a different charge interpretation.
- A generic higher-rank frame or Grassmannian target may have a non-integer or
  non-\(\mathbb{Z}\) classification.
- A Procrustes or matrix holonomy is generally continuous.

The existing sampled-winding integer remains local to its declared discrete
interpolation. Topology promotion additionally requires that permitted
perturbations cannot change the integer without crossing the singular set or
closing a declared identifiability gap.

## 6. Graph-construction-family invariance

Deterministic construction under one protocol is reproducibility, not
construction invariance.

A topology-promotion protocol must preregister a genuinely distinct admissible
family, initially including at least:

- mutual-\(k\)-nearest-neighbor construction;
- fixed-\(\epsilon\) metric-radius construction;
- shared-nearest-neighbor or neighborhood-overlap construction.

Metric changes are a separate null so metric and adjacency are not silently
confounded. Family scales are selected without subject winding outcomes and
are matched on declared nuisance targets such as common vertex support,
component coverage, two-core coverage, and an edge or degree budget.

If a graph estimates the local field as well as supplying cycles, the required
design is crossed:

\[
\text{field-estimation graph } A
\times
\text{cycle-construction graph } B.
\]

Agreement only on diagonal \(A=B\) cells can be a shared-construction artifact.

If core localization uses a neighborhood graph, it is also a declared nuisance
axis. The estimator either declares `graph_free`, inherits and binds the
field-estimation graph, or adds a matched-support core graph \(C\) and tests
\(C\times A\times B\). Core stability cannot be inferred from field/cycle
agreement while its own graph construction remains hidden.

The comparison is over a matched support or homology class, not whichever cycle
preserves the desired result. Outcomes are:

- `pass`: every required, genuinely distinct and evaluable family or crossed
  cell preserves the frozen class and declared observable;
- `fail_graph_dependence`: adequately supported families disagree;
- `insufficient`: a class cannot be matched, cycle support is absent, the field
  is unresolved, or the families are not genuinely distinct;
- `invalid_protocol`: graph, scale, center, plane, or threshold was selected
  using the observed subject result.

No required family is dropped after its result is observed. Worst-case
supported behavior controls promotion; an average cannot hide one failure.
Every required stratum must also bind its evaluation unit, attempted and
evaluable counts, maximum abstention, and minimum coverage. Floors that turn
hard cases into unevaluable cases cannot manufacture a pass.

## 7. Branched claim taxonomy

The project uses a branched claim taxonomy rather than treating every
invariance result as topology.

| Label | Meaning | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 0 | analogy | motivating language only |
| Level 1G | geometric observable | deterministic support, transport, loop, or holonomy quantity |
| Level 1D | defect-field observable | substrate-bound order-parameter field and its bound core diagnostic, or sampled winding of its declared interpolation |
| Level 2G | invariant geometric candidate | Level 1G plus gauge, architecture, deformation, and matched geometric nulls |
| Level 2T | topological-defect candidate | Level 1D plus core independence, charge resolution, matched-class and graph-family invariance |
| Level 3 | semantic and causal | held-out semantic prediction and selective causal intervention |

Level 2G does not imply Level 2T. Level 2T does not imply semantics. A run may
support one branch, both branches, or neither. Some Level 1G support
diagnostics have no promotion path by themselves; their role is to mark
identifiability or block a later field claim.

## 8. Experimental typed boundaries

The following names describe experimental contracts, not stable public APIs.
Their metadata-only v0.1 schemas are implemented in
`spirallens.instrument_contracts`; implementing those schemas does not mean
that an estimator has emitted any of them:

- `SubstrateBinding`
- `SupportDiagnostic`
- `GeometricFieldEstimate`
- `OrderParameterSpec`
- `OrderParameterField`
- `CoreScore`
- `CoreCandidate`
- `GroundTruthAnchor`
- `GraphConstructionSpec`
- `CandidateGraph`
- `EdgeConnection`
- `LoopEstimate`
- `GraphFamilyNullProtocol`
- `GraphFamilyNullResult`
- `CalibrationSelectionDecision`
- `CalibrationConfirmationResult`
- `InstrumentBundleManifest`
- `SubjectProtocolManifest`

They should be persisted as sidecars bound to the immutable candidate-ledger
and substrate identities. They do not belong in `neighbors/`, whose state-only
retrieval boundary remains unchanged.

`LoopEstimate` is a discriminated union. Its geometry variant binds a
`GeometricFieldEstimate` and connection without requiring a core or order
parameter. Its defect variant binds an `OrderParameterField`; it binds a
`CoreCandidate` only for a localized-defect claim. A supplied
`GroundTruthAnchor` remains calibration metadata and cannot substitute for an
inferred core.

The v0.1 contract implementation provides canonical hashing, exact field sets,
tamper rejection, row-order bindings, explicit
`pass|fail|insufficient|not_run` states, and byte-replay tests. Its
closed-integrity bundle boundary resolves exact artifact and payload
identities, rejects unreachable or cyclic indexed artifacts, streams opaque
payload bytes for length and digest checks, and validates the selected typed
metadata joins. It does not decode payload values, recompute row identities,
or qualify an instrument. A separate authorization-bound numeric consumer may
retain selected descriptors during that same secure validation transaction,
strictly decode their numeric snapshots, derive row identity from content, and
verify closed value relations. That consumer does not change the ordinary
loader's scope and does not qualify a referent, graph, or instrument. A subject
protocol still cannot depend on either path until synthetic qualification
D0-D8 is complete. See
[P0 Hypothesis and Artifact Contracts](P0_HYPOTHESIS_AND_ARTIFACT_CONTRACTS.md).

## 9. Historical and experimental consequences

Under this frame:

- analytic winding phantoms qualify winding mathematics for supplied fields;
  they do not define a Pythia field;
- activation atlases define observation substrates, not semantic or phase
  fields;
- high-cosine/divergent-update pairs remain structural retrieval candidates,
  not cores or phase shifts;
- neighbor audits qualify retrieval support only;
- continuous holonomy remains a geometry-branch observable;
- sampled winding on caller-supplied values remains a declared-interpolation
  observable;
- architecture and gauge controls remain necessary but cannot create an order
  parameter;
- SAE and semantic tests remain downstream of structural discovery.

The detailed artifact-by-artifact reading is frozen in the interpretation
ledger.

## 10. Gate before the next subject experiment

No new Pythia subject experiment is prepared until all of the following exist:

1. an independently reviewed substrate and order-parameter contract;
2. at least two competing field hypotheses or a written justification for one;
3. analytic positive, negative, zero-core, off-core, alias, gauge, and
   non-orientability controls;
4. a graph-family and crossed-design contract;
5. a charge-blind `CoreScore` and `CoreCandidate` path bound to the same
   order-parameter field, graph-accounted, distinct from supplied ground truth,
   and frozen before loop readout;
6. explicit failure, coverage, abstention, aggregation, and support rules;
7. a calibration-selection decision sealed before hidden confirmation;
8. a split and leakage policy that prevents prior subject outcomes from
   selecting the confirmatory field or graph;
9. replayable artifacts and an adversarially reviewed freeze plan;
10. a future subject-access manifest that reveals no subject-derived values
    before its own review and freeze.

The preparation sequence is defined in
[Next Experiment Preparation](NEXT_EXPERIMENT_PREPARATION.md).

### Public-example engineering carve-out

One narrow real-model lane remains available before D8 because it tests the
observation apparatus rather than a scientific subject. A tracked
`public_example_engineering` protocol may load the exact Pythia-70M revision
bound by the public ContextBank and persist a bounded activation atlas only
when every selected context has `role=example`, the bank has
`claim_eligible=false`, and the protocol freezes CPU/float32 capture, exact
rows, source and model-file identities, resource ceilings, and an output ID.

This lane records the access facts positively: the model was loaded and
activation values were persisted. It is nevertheless ineligible for
scientific claims and is not a subject protocol, subject preparation, or
progress on D0-D8. Its only authorized consumer is atlas integrity validation.
Candidate extraction, neighbor audit, instrument-bundle conversion, graph,
field, core, loop, holonomy, winding, semantic, SAE, and integer consumers
must reject it before reading activation arrays. Expanding that allowlist is a
change to this frame, not an interpretation of a successful smoke run.
The resulting receipt is a committed-code procedural attestation, not
cryptographic proof against deliberate local artifact forgery.
